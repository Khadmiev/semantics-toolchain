# Evidence dossiers

The article names four failure classes of a probabilistic compiler of
semantics and tells one incident for each, plus a second echelon it treats
briefly. Every incident comes from the author's own toolchain being built
through itself. These dossiers are the contemporaneous internal records
behind the telling: entries written at the time, in the author's own
working store, by the development agent or the operator. They are not
independent evidence of the underlying events, and they do not claim to be.

## What a dossier is, and what it is not

Each dossier is a self-contained exhibit:

- a **context header** written for the reader: what happened, when, in
  which review, and what the toolchain changed in response;
- the **record(s) verbatim**, in the language the record was
  written in. Most are Russian; one is English. Nothing in a quoted record
  is altered except redactions, and every redaction is visible as
  `[redacted: reason]`;
- for a Russian record, a **marked English translation** beside it. The
  translation is the author's agent's and is not part of the record;
- a **provenance line** per record: the store it comes from, the record's
  type and identifier, its creation time as the store recorded it, its
  status at export.

Curation is acknowledged. The author chose which records to publish and
which passages to quote; the records themselves were written at the time,
by the development agent or the operator, as working entries in a private
store. They carry no external timestamp: the creation times are the store's
own, reproduced here as recorded, and a reader cannot verify them
independently. What the dossiers corroborate is that the quoted lines and
dates exist in that store as quoted.

The store is the author's private knowledge graph, the same kind of store
this toolchain provides. Identifiers are reproduced so that the author can
be asked about a specific record; they resolve nowhere public.

## Language

The repository is English-only with this single exception: a record is
quoted in the language it happened in. A translated record would be a
retelling, and a retelling is not a record.

## Licence of third-party material

The records quote the operator and the operator's agents. Where a record
quotes a model's output (a critic pass, a development message), the output
was produced in the author's own sessions under terms that assign it to the
user. No third-party text is quoted. See `NOTICE` for the general clause.
