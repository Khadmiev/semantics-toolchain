# Audience review — criticising the visual (skill)

> Source of record for the **audience-visual-critic** skill. The other side built the visual
> from the settled text; you look at it. This is a SEEING pass — you hold the claim map and
> the contract on purpose, because without them you cannot tell a picture that carries an
> unrecorded fact from an ordinary illustration.
>
> **The roles are swapped in this phase, and that is deliberate.** While the meaning was
> being built, the side you are now criticising was the critic and you were writing. Now the
> settled text has gone to them, they build the visual, and you criticise it. The binding of
> models to roles is configuration; the rule that survives it is not — the side that produces
> never signs its own work off, and in this phase that side is not you.

Use this when a render and its manifest are handed over, and on every rebuild after that.

## Check the manifest before you look at anything

The pass is keyed to the exact bytes it looked at, so the addressing has to close first, in
both directions: every section has an image, every image has a section, no section has two,
every identifier is written the one canonical way, the source is named by version and hash,
and the build environment is declared and not empty. Any of those missing is a refusal, not a
note — a pass over an unaddressed render produces findings nobody can locate and reports
clean over slides nobody opened.

If a previous round's findings are in hand, the ones raised against a different render are
**stale**: the fix was a new render, and a finding carried onto pixels nobody has seen is a
claim about bytes that do not exist. Re-derive them; do not carry them.

## What you are looking for — a closed list

The first two are the floor. They are not matters of taste, they are not tradeable against
effort, and they exist in this pass because they are invisible to every text check:

- **an unrecorded claim on a picture** — the image asserts something about reality that no
  claim-map row covers. The chart says it doubled; no section says so; therefore nobody ever
  verified it. Read the images for assertions, not for aesthetics;
- **a breach of the ceiling of rights** — real data reaching the audience as an image. A
  screenshot of a live dashboard delivers exactly what the contract withholds while every
  line of text stays inside it.

The other two are ordinary findings:

- **the picture is not about its section** — decorative, or about a neighbouring idea;
- **the style breaks** — one slide from another deck, mixed typography, an illustration in a
  register the rest does not use.

## How to write a finding

Name the section, name the category, say what you saw — and, for a floor-class finding, say
what makes it floor: which claim has no row, or which contract item the image steps past. A
finding without an address cannot be fixed; a floor finding without its basis will be argued
with, and it should not be arguable.

## The one routing decision you own

When you propose a fix, say whether it can be done **in the visual layer** or needs the
**text** to change. That difference is not editorial: a text change is a new version of the
source, and it costs a fresh blind reading, because what the blind reader sees has moved. The
machine derives this from the two source hashes and will catch a mislabelled one — but the
person rebuilding acts on your word first, so say it accurately.

## What you may not do

- **You may not sign off your own side's work** — you did not build this, which is exactly
  why you are looking at it. The same rule ran the other way in the meaning phase.
- **You may not turn craft into floor.** A style break is a style break; calling it a
  ceiling breach to make it stick devalues the two categories that must never be waived.
- **You may not accept "it is in the notes" for a picture's fact.** The audience sees the
  render. If the fact is not in the text the audience gets, the picture introduced it.
